How it's built

The problem. Chatbots that answer everything are a liability: they invent prices and policies. Chatbots that answer nothing are a phone tree. Small businesses need the middle: instant answers from their own facts, and honesty about everything else.

The build. n8n with a chat or WhatsApp trigger, code-based memory and guards, and Gemini under a prompt that forbids inventing facts. Contact details are only accepted if the customer actually typed them. Every question is logged with an answered-from-the-sheet flag, so the owner sees exactly where the sheet has gaps.

Where it started. The first version ran on Telegram, with production runs of 8.6 and 9.6 seconds tested from a real phone: the opening-hours question answered exactly from the sheet, the yoga-mats question honestly deflected.

Behind the build

The hardest thing I've built is a bot that says “I don't know.”

Answering is easy: any AI will answer anything. Stopping is the work. Admitting the gap, and handing the question to a human. Most bots skip that step, and their customers pay for it.

Would you trust a bot that never says “I don't know”?

About

I build for the failure case first.

Most automation fails in one of two ways. It breaks quietly, and nobody notices until a month of leads has gone missing. Or it works too well: it answers a pricing question it should never have touched, in a voice that leaves your customer feeling handled by a machine.

So every system here has a designed path for “I don't know”: the bot that admits it and emails you the exact question, the classifier that hands anything sensitive to a human, the content engine where nothing publishes until you approve it. Then I write the voice, because a workflow that runs perfectly and sounds like a robot has only solved half the problem.
