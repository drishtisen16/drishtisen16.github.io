Newest · RAG chat assistant · live

How a small business answers Telegram messages at midnight — from its own docs, never guessing

Problem
Chatbots that answer everything are a liability: they invent prices and policies. Chatbots that answer nothing are a phone tree. Small businesses need the middle: instant answers from THEIR facts, and honesty about everything else.
Build
Customers message a Telegram bot. Every question is scored against a knowledge base that lives in a plain Google Sheet — the owner edits it like any spreadsheet. The AI answers strictly from the top-matching entries, personalized by name. If the docs don't cover it, the bot says so, promises a human follow-up, and the owner gets an email naming the exact missing question. Every conversation is logged with a found-in-docs flag.
Tools
n8n (Telegram trigger, code-based retrieval, IF routing), Gemini API with a grounding prompt that forbids inventing facts and a designed failure path — plus automatic retry, because production AI must survive provider hiccups. Gmail, Google Sheets.
Result
Live production runs of 8.6 and 9.6 seconds, tested from a real phone: the hours question answered exactly from the sheet; the yoga-mats question honestly deflected — and the knowledge gap emailed to the owner within seconds.
Flagship · Automation + Ghostwriting

How one blog post becomes a week of LinkedIn content — in the client's own voice

Problem
Coaches and founders have plenty to say and no time to say it. Generic AI content tools "solve" this by making everyone sound like the same robot — which quietly costs them the trust they post to build.
Build
A webhook receives one source (blog post, podcast transcript, voice note) plus a voice profile I write from the client's real writing. An AI engine drafts 3 LinkedIn posts, a newsletter section, and 5 short posts under strict JSON validation, emails the client a review digest, and logs all 9 pieces to a content calendar marked "Draft for review." Nothing publishes without approval.
Tools
n8n (webhook, code, split, append), Gemini API — chosen because a small client's volume fits the free tier, and costs matter at small-business scale — Gmail API, Google Sheets.
Result
Latest live run: one 250-word finance brief in → 9 publish-ready pieces out in 18.754 seconds, digest delivered, calendar filled. Runs on the client's own accounts.

AI assistant · live 24/7

How customer messages get answered in 9.7 seconds — around the clock

Problem
The average small business misses most of its inquiries. Messages sit overnight; buyers move on. Full auto-reply bots are worse — they answer pricing negotiations and complaints they should never touch.
Build
Every incoming message hits a webhook. AI classifies it — general, booking, complex — and answers routine questions instantly in the business's tone. Anything sensitive is escalated to the owner with a ready-to-edit draft instead of being auto-sent. Every inquiry is logged with category and timestamp.
Tools
n8n (webhook, IF routing), Gemini API with a strict-JSON classification prompt and a fallback that routes anything unparseable to a human — the failure mode is designed, not hoped away. Gmail, Google Sheets.
Result
Live production run: 9.756 seconds from inquiry received to personalized reply sent and logged. Zero sensitive messages auto-answered.
Lead capture · live

How form leads stop going cold: logged, alerted, answered in under four seconds

Problem
Around 80% of leads go cold when the first reply takes more than five minutes. Most small businesses take hours — because form submissions land in an inbox nobody is watching.
Build
Any form posts to a webhook. The lead is appended to a Google Sheet, the owner gets a notification, and the lead gets a personalized confirmation — all before they've closed the tab. Works with Google Forms, website forms, or WhatsApp-to-form flows.
Tools
n8n (webhook, set, append), Gmail API, Google Sheets. Chose n8n over Zapier because per-task pricing punishes exactly the lead volume this is built to create.
Result
Live production run: 3.933 seconds end-to-end, every node green. The lead hears back before a human could have opened the email.
Carousel generation · published

How a LinkedIn carousel gets written, designed and rendered without opening Canva

Problem
Carousels outperform plain posts on LinkedIn, and almost nobody keeps making them — because every slide is a separate design job. Writing is fast; laying out ten slides by hand is what kills the habit.
Build
A brief goes in. An AI writer drafts the slide copy, the workflow splits it into individual slide jobs, and each slide is rendered into a finished image by an HTML-to-image API. The set is assembled into an email digest, and every slide is logged to a Carousel Calendar sheet with its image URL and a Draft for review status — same approval gate as the content engine. Nothing publishes itself.
Tools
n8n (webhook, code, split, append), Gemini API for the slide copy, htmlcsstoimage for rendering, Gmail API, Google Sheets.
Result
Published and running on my own account. Latest run logged 6 slides, each written, rendered to a finished image, and filed in the calendar as Draft for review.
