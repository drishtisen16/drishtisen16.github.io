Newest · AI front desk · live — try it

How a small business answers customer questions at midnight — from its own info sheet, never guessing

Problem
Chatbots that answer everything are a liability: they invent prices and policies. Chatbots that answer nothing are a phone tree. Small businesses need the middle: instant answers from THEIR facts, and honesty about everything else.
Build
A customer opens a chat link — any browser, no app, no signup. Every question is answered from a knowledge base that lives in a plain Google Sheet the owner edits like any spreadsheet. If the sheet doesn't cover it, the assistant says so and offers to pass the question on. When someone wants to book, it collects their name, contact and preferred time, saves them to the sheet and emails the owner. Every question is logged with an answered-from-the-sheet flag, so the owner sees exactly where the sheet has gaps.
Tools
n8n (public chat trigger, code-based memory and guards, IF routing), Gemini API with a grounding prompt that forbids inventing facts, a contact check that only accepts details the visitor actually typed, and automatic retry — because production AI must survive provider hiccups. Gmail, Google Sheets.
Result
The first version ran on Telegram: live production runs of 8.6 and 9.6 seconds, tested from a real phone — the hours question answered exactly from the sheet, the yoga-mats question honestly deflected, the knowledge gap emailed to the owner within seconds. The web version is live now, for a fictional gym you can test yourself: try the live demo →
Flagship · Automation + Ghostwriting

How one blog post becomes a week of LinkedIn content — in the client's own voice

Problem
Coaches and founders have plenty to say and no time to say it. Generic AI content tools "solve" this by making everyone sound like the same robot — which quietly costs them the trust they post to build.
Build
A webhook receives one source (blog post, podcast transcript, voice note) plus a voice profile I write from the client's real writing. An AI engine drafts 3 LinkedIn posts, a newsletter section, and 5 short posts under strict JSON validation, emails the client a review digest, and logs all 9 pieces to a content calendar marked "Draft for review." Nothing publishes without approval.
Tools
n8n (webhook, code, split, append), Gemini API — chosen because a small client's volume fits the free tier, and costs matter at small-business scale — Gmail API, Google Sheets.
Result
Latest live run: one 250-word finance brief in → 9 publish-ready pieces out in 18.754 seconds, digest delivered, calendar filled. Runs on the client's own accounts.
Lead capture · live

How form leads stop going cold: logged, alerted, answered in under four seconds

Problem
Firms that tried to contact a web lead within an hour were nearly seven times as likely to qualify it as those that tried even an hour later — yet in an audit of 2,241 U.S. companies, only 37% responded within the hour (Harvard Business Review, 2011). The usual reason: form submissions land in an inbox nobody is watching.
Build
Any form posts to a webhook. The lead is appended to a Google Sheet, the owner gets a notification, and the lead gets a personalized confirmation — all before they've closed the tab. Works with Google Forms, website forms, or WhatsApp-to-form flows.
Tools
n8n (webhook, set, append), Gmail API, Google Sheets. Chose n8n over Zapier because per-task pricing punishes exactly the lead volume this is built to create.
Result
Live production run: 3.933 seconds end-to-end, every node green. The lead hears back before a human could have opened the email.

About

How I build, and why

Most automation fails in one of two ways. It breaks quietly — and nobody notices until a month of leads has gone missing. Or it works too well: it answers a pricing question it should never have touched, in a voice that leaves your customer feeling handled by a machine.

So I build for the failure case first. Every system on this page has a designed path for I don't know — the bot that admits it and emails you the exact question it couldn't answer, the classifier that hands anything sensitive to a human, the content engine where nothing publishes until you approve it. Then I write the voice profile, because a workflow that runs perfectly and sounds like a robot has only solved half the problem.
