Case A · Own business · In production

Cold email with brakes

Sender · daily
Read settings→Off-switch on?→Skip opt-outs→Send, paced→Mark SENT / SKIPPED→Summary to owner
Watcher · every 30 min
Read new mail→Classify→Reply → update queue, alertOpt-out → suppression listWarning → switch sender off
Teal outline = guardrail
Problem

Cold email from a business mailbox can go wrong fast — a bad batch, a reply nobody sees, an opt-out that gets emailed again.

Result

No one on the opt-out list can be emailed, every reply is seen within half an hour, and the system can pause its own sending without me in the loop.

When it breaks

The happy path is the easy part. This is what each system does when something goes wrong, and where the evidence lands.

FailureWhat the system doesWhere it shows
A warning reply arrivesThe inbox watcher routes it as a brake: switches the sender off in the settings sheet and emails me why. The sender checks that switch before every run.Settings sheet · brake alert
Someone opts outThey're added to the suppression list within 30 minutes. Every send run reads that list first and skips anyone on it.Suppression sheet
A send run stops midwayEach row is marked SENDING before the email goes and SENT after, so an interrupted row stays visible instead of disappearing. A summary email follows every run.Send queue · run summary
An API call failsCalls to Gemini, Google and other APIs retry on error. The call-list workflow checks the Apify run's status before it pulls any results.n8n execution log
The AI returns something unusableIts output is parsed and checked before use. Anything that doesn't parse goes to a person, never to a customer.Owner alert
The AI writes something off-toneOutreach and welcome emails land as Gmail drafts. A person reads them before anything is sent.Gmail drafts
A workflow fails quietlyA separate watchdog workflow flags failed runs, so a silent failure becomes a message.Watchdog alert

Three rules every build follows

Answer only from approved information

Assistants read from the owner's sheet or knowledge base, and say so when a question isn't covered.

A person before anything sensitive

Sensitive messages and first-touch emails wait for human approval.

Every run leaves a record

Execution logs, status columns and summary emails — so problems can be traced.

How I write to customers

Hi [Name], thanks for checking. Your refund for order [#] was issued on [date] — I can see it on our side. Banks usually take [3–5] business days to show it. If it isn't there by [date], reply here and I'll chase it with our payments team myself, so you won't need to explain it again.

Macro 2 · Locked out of account

Hi [Name], sorry you're locked out. For your security I can't change account details over chat, but this gets you back in: go to [link], choose “Forgot password” and use the email on the account. If nothing arrives within 10 minutes, check spam, then reply “no email” and I'll take it from there.

States the limit once, then gives steps and a fallback.

Hi [Name], you've had to contact us twice about this, and that's on us. Here's what I've done: [action taken]. Here's what happens next: [next step] by [date and time]. I've added a note to your case so whoever you speak to next sees the full history.

Owns the miss, skips the scripted apology, ends on one concrete step.

Work samples

Two decision memos

Fictional cases · written for this portfolio · not WEJOY material · based on common clauses in public community guidelines

Memo 1 · “It's just banter”Decision · Action
ReportA user reports repeated insults in a public voice-room chat. The reported user says they're friends and it's banter. EvidenceFour messages in ten minutes, all aimed at one person. That person filed the report. PolicyHarassment: repeated, targeted insults at an individual. DecisionRemove the messages and warn the reported user. ReasoningBanter needs both sides to treat it as banter. The target reporting it is the clearest signal they don't. Repetition plus a single target meets the clause. EscalationNone. Note added to the account so a repeat is easy to spot.
Memo 2 · Graphic image, shared as newsDecision · Escalate
ReportA post shares a screenshot of a news report showing an injured person, captioned to raise awareness. PolicyGraphic violence is restricted. The guideline I'm working from doesn't define a news or awareness exception. DecisionEscalate. Limit visibility while it's reviewed, if the platform allows. ReasoningRemoving it may suppress legitimate news. Leaving it up shows graphic content with no warning. Where the policy is silent, the call belongs to the policy owners — my job is to hand them a clean case. EscalationTo the policy team with the post, the clause, both readings, and my recommendation: keep it behind a warning screen.
Work sample

Scoring two model answers

Sample · written for this portfolio · not client material · scale 1–5

User prompt

“My package says delivered but it's not here. What do I do?”

Response A

“Sorry to hear that! Packages marked delivered almost always are. Check with your neighbours. If you still can't find it, you'll get a full refund automatically within 24 hours.”

Response B · Preferred

“That's frustrating. A few quick checks: look around your door and mailbox, ask anyone at home, and see if the carrier left a delivery photo. Parcels are sometimes marked delivered a little early. If it hasn't turned up by tomorrow, contact the seller or carrier with your order number — they can open a missing-package claim.”

CriterionABNote
Accuracy25A invents a refund policy it can't know.
Helpfulness25B gives ordered steps and a clear next action.
Tone34“Almost always are” quietly doubts the user.
Made-up claimsFailPassA promises an outcome on the company's behalf.
Verdict

B, clearly. A sounds friendlier at first glance, but its refund promise is a hallucinated policy. A customer acting on it would be let down twice. One invented claim outweighs any gain in warmth.

Writing

Ghostwriting since July 2022 for coaches, consultants and founders — published under their names, in their voice.

Teardown · one rewrite, three decisions

My own post · LinkedIn · 2026
1
Cut the borrowed words

“Passionate”, “empowering” and “unlock their potential” sit in thousands of coach bios. They tell the reader nothing about this coach.

2
Swap a promise for an observation

“Promoted for being great at the job, then handed a team with zero instructions” is something only a working coach would notice.

3
End on a door, not a list

“Here's the first one I make them have…” pulls the reader into the next line instead of five interchangeable tips.
